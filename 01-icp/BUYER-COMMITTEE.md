## Why the committee matters

Even in a ten-person company, a purchase decision rarely sits with one person. The founder gets the pitch, the operations lead does the research, the bookkeeper raises the budget question, and the office manager has to actually use the thing. A marketing system that only speaks to the founder will lose the deal to a vendor that also spoke to the other three.

## Committee roles

### Economic Buyer

- **Typical title:** [CEO, Owner, CFO, VP of X]
- **What they own:** The signature. The budget. The final yes.
- **What they care about:** ROI, risk, strategic fit.
- **What they fear:** Buying a lemon, getting called out in a board meeting, missing a number.
- **Questions they will ask:** What is the payback period. Who else like us is already using this. What happens if it does not work.
- **Preferred content:** Case studies with financial outcomes, executive summaries, references.
- **How often they engage:** Late in the cycle, short conversations, high stakes.

### Champion

- **Typical title:** [Marketing Director, Ops Director, Head of X]
- **What they own:** Pushing the deal forward inside the company.
- **What they care about:** Looking smart. Solving the problem on their plate. Getting a win.
- **What they fear:** Recommending the wrong vendor and owning the fallout.
- **Questions they will ask:** How do I pitch this to my boss. What does onboarding look like. Can I see a demo environment.
- **Preferred content:** Pitch-ready slide decks, one-pagers, ROI calculators, comparison pages, founder calls.
- **How often they engage:** Constantly. Your champion is your inside sales rep.

### End User

- **Typical title:** [Coordinator, Specialist, Analyst, Field Tech]
- **What they own:** The day-to-day use of what you sell.
- **What they care about:** Whether it makes their job easier or harder.
- **What they fear:** Another tool to learn, another password to remember.
- **Questions they will ask:** Do I have to change my workflow. How long until I get productive. What do I do when it breaks.
- **Preferred content:** Product walkthroughs, short videos, help docs, onboarding checklists.
- **How often they engage:** Daily during trial, rarely during purchase.

### Technical Evaluator

- **Typical title:** [IT Manager, CTO, Head of Engineering, Systems Admin]
- **What they own:** The technical fit.
- **What they care about:** Security, integrations, data ownership, scalability.
- **What they fear:** A vendor that creates more support tickets than it solves.
- **Questions they will ask:** How do you handle SSO. What is your data retention policy. Do you have a SOC 2 or equivalent.
- **Preferred content:** Security pages, integration docs, API references, status page, architecture diagrams.
- **How often they engage:** One deep call mid-cycle, one checklist review at close.

### Skeptic

- **Typical title:** [Anyone senior with battle scars]
- **What they own:** The veto.
- **What they care about:** Not getting burned again.
- **What they fear:** Another agency, another vendor, another promise.
- **Questions they will ask:** What makes you different. Who have you fired. Show me a deal that went wrong.
- **Preferred content:** Honest comparison pages, recorded customer interviews, handwritten references, a pilot program.
- **How often they engage:** Once, loudly, late in the cycle.

### Finance and Procurement

- **Typical title:** [Controller, CFO, Procurement Manager]
- **What they own:** The contract and the payment terms.
- **What they care about:** Price, payment terms, cancellation, liability.
- **What they fear:** Auto-renew surprises, scope creep, audit risk.
- **Questions they will ask:** What is the pricing page. Can we pay annually. What is the exit clause.
- **Preferred content:** Transparent pricing page, sample contract, standard MSA, invoice terms.
- **How often they engage:** Once, at the end.

### Legal and Compliance

- **Typical title:** [General Counsel, Compliance Officer, DPO]
- **What they own:** The redlines.
- **What they care about:** Data processing, IP ownership, liability caps, indemnification.
- **What they fear:** Signing something that creates exposure.
- **Questions they will ask:** Can you provide a DPA. Where is the data stored. What insurance do you carry.
- **Preferred content:** Published DPA, Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, insurance certificates.
- **How often they engage:** Once, formally, after the champion has a verbal yes.

### Influencer

- **Typical title:** [Peer at another company, industry voice, consultant]
- **What they own:** Trust. Not the decision, but the opinion the decision-maker listens to.
- **What they care about:** Being seen as the person who knew about the good vendor first.
- **What they fear:** Recommending a vendor that makes them look bad.
- **Questions they will ask:** Who else is using you. What do you stand for. Do you actually get it.
- **Preferred content:** Thought-leadership articles, founder-led content, speaking appearances, community presence.
- **How often they engage:** In the background. They shape the deal before you even know it exists.

## Worked example

At Northstar Home Services, the buying committee looks like this.

- **Economic buyer:** The owner, Ray Alvarez. Signs anything above $5,000 per month.
- **Champion:** Brenda Alvarez, Ops Director. Finds vendors, builds the internal case, pushes the decision.
- **End user:** The dispatch team and the lead intake coordinator. They use whatever tool the marketing vendor gives them to track leads.
- **Technical evaluator:** An outsourced IT contractor. Reviews integrations with ServiceTitan and QuickBooks.
- **Skeptic:** Ray's brother, who runs the service side. Has been burned by two agencies and will push back on day one.
- **Finance:** A part-time bookkeeper. Wants a line-item invoice and a 30-day exit clause.
- **Influencer:** A peer in the local home services association who recently hired a marketing agency and is happy with them.

## Feeding this asset to an AI agent

```text
When writing a multi-touch sequence, a sales page, or a proposal, load /01-icp/BUYER-COMMITTEE.md.

Before drafting, identify which roles will see the asset.

- For a homepage, assume Champion and Influencer land first.
- For a pricing page, assume Economic Buyer and Finance read carefully.
- For a security page, write for Technical Evaluator and Legal.
- For a nurture sequence, rotate assets so every role sees at least one piece built for them.

If the asset mixes audiences, write it for the Champion and give the Economic Buyer, Finance, and Technical Evaluator dedicated sections or sidebar links.
```
