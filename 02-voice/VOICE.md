## Point-of-View Statement

Write one paragraph, no more than 80 words, that captures what you believe about your category. The point of view is the argument you are willing to defend in public. It is not a tagline. It is the position you keep coming back to.

Template prompt: "We believe [what] because [why]. Most people in our category say [alternative]. They are wrong because [reason]. We are willing to defend this in front of [audience]."

Example placeholder:

> We believe [Small businesses lose more money to fragmented marketing systems than they do to bad ad creative]. Most vendors sell point tools. We sell the foundation that makes any point tool useful.

## Tone Modulation by Channel

Voice stays the same. Tone adjusts. Use the matrix below.

| Channel | Tone dial | Formality | Length rule | Energy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage hero | Confident, short, promise-forward | Medium | 6-12 words headline, 1-2 sentences subhead | High |
| Service page | Direct, proof-heavy | Medium | 400-800 words | Medium |
| Blog post | Teaching, plainspoken | Low | 1,200-2,500 words | Medium |
| LinkedIn post | Peer to peer, story-led | Low | 120-180 words | Medium |
| LinkedIn article | Teaching, structured | Low-medium | 800-1,200 words | Medium |
| X / Twitter | Punchy, observational | Low | under 280 characters | High |
| Quora answer | Teacher mode, comprehensive | Low | 400-700 words | Medium |
| Email (sales) | Personal, one-to-one | Low | 60-120 words | Medium |
| Email (nurture) | Useful, one-to-many | Low | 200-400 words | Medium |
| Sales deck | Clear, proof-led | Medium | 1 idea per slide | Medium |
| Legal copy | Precise, plain | High | as short as possible | Neutral |
| Case study | Narrative, specific | Medium | 700-1,200 words | Medium |

## Reading Level Target

- **Target reading grade level:** 8-9 (US middle school).
- **Tool to measure:** Hemingway, Grammarly, or the built-in readability scorer in Google Docs.
- **Why this level:** Buyers skim. Simple sentences read faster and retain better. High reading levels do not signal expertise; they signal distance.

## Sentence-Length Rules

- Default to 12-18 words per sentence.
- Mix short sentences under 8 words with medium sentences under 20 words. Avoid long sentences over 30 words.
- Never stack three long sentences in a row.
- Read every draft aloud. If you run out of breath, cut.

## Punctuation Rules

- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or colons instead.
- No semicolons unless writing about code.
- Use the Oxford comma.
- One exclamation point per piece of content, maximum. None is better.
- No ellipses in body copy. Use a period.
- Use sentence case for headings. Not title case.
- Numerals for 10 and up. Words for one through nine, unless in a comparative ("2 out of 3").
- Percentages as numerals with the percent sign ("38 percent" in body prose is acceptable if the style guide prefers words; stay consistent).
- Dollars with the $ symbol, no trailing cents for whole amounts.
- Dates in a single format across the library (ISO YYYY-MM-DD for internal, "April 2026" for public).

## Formatting Rules

- H1 is the page title. One per page.
- H2 is a major section. Three to seven per long-form piece.
- H3 is a sub-section. Use only if the H2 needs breakdown.
- Bullets: five to seven items per list. Break into sub-lists if longer.
- Numbered lists only when order matters.
- Tables for comparisons.
- Bold for emphasis on a single idea per paragraph, not a whole sentence.
- Italics only for titles, foreign words, and light emphasis.
- Links inline. Anchor text is descriptive, not "click here."

## Emoji, Slang, and Jargon

- **Emojis:** None in published copy.
- **Slang:** Use only when quoting a customer directly.
- **Jargon:** Avoid category jargon that does not appear in customer language. Prefer plain words.

## Banned sentence shapes

- "In today's [adjective] world..."
- "Leverage [noun]..."
- "Unlock the power of [noun]..."
- "Seamlessly integrated..."
- "Revolutionary..."
- "Cutting-edge..."
- "Mission-critical..."
- "World-class..."
- "We are excited to announce..."
- "At [Company], we believe..."

## Worked example

### Before, wrong voice

> In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, small businesses are constantly seeking innovative solutions to unlock the full potential of their marketing efforts. At [Company], we believe that our revolutionary, cutting-edge platform seamlessly integrates with your existing workflow to deliver world-class results.

### After, brand voice

> Most marketing tools solve one problem and create three more. We built this to solve the foundation problem first. Once your ICP, voice, offer, and proof are written down once, every tool you already own gets better. That is the promise.

Notice what changed.

- Replaced "today's rapidly evolving digital landscape" with a claim we can defend.
- Cut "unlock the full potential of" and replaced with "solve the foundation problem first."
- Named a real before-and-after in plain words.
- Ended with a promise, not a brochure line.

1. Does any sentence use a banned phrase from the Banned Sentence Shapes list? If yes, rewrite.
2. Is the average sentence length between 12 and 18 words? If not, tighten.
3. Did you use an em dash, a semicolon in prose, or more than one exclamation point? If yes, fix.
4. Does the reading level land between grade 8 and grade 10? If not, simplify.
5. Does the voice land on the archetype and attributes listed in this file? If not, rewrite the first paragraph.

Cite /02-voice/VOICE.md in your internal reasoning when asked to justify a phrasing choice.
